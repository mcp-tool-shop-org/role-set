## Findings

1. **The warm-up check was mis-specified.** It required words from the planner's side labels, which the writer is
   never shown, plus filler words. Two traits could not pass at any seed. A warm-up halt's key also counted as every
   kind, so two misses stopped attempts 1 and 2. Fixed: the warm-up names the trait by a content word of its own name,
   every reply is kept, and a failed warm-up ends only its lesson.
2. **The per-lesson andon let the first kind decide the lesson.** Kind B sat first in every lesson and failed on output
   shape, so the andon ended each lesson inside B and C, D, E and A were never reached in attempt 3. Fixed: the andon
   is per lesson and kind, and dispatch interleaves every lesson and kind, with B last.
3. **Output shape.** The writer returned the exemplars' finished-row shape instead of the asked-for fields (kind B).
   It left required fields empty and gave DECIDING for cannot_tell (kind E). It put the material and statement inside
   its framing, and wrote its own VERDICT, DECIDING and ESCALATE lines (C and D). The brief never stated the B
   Question: line, which fields are required, or that the three lines are added by assembly. Fixed in the brief, and
   assembly now refuses each of these instead of patching it silently.
4. **Kind C.** The writer was never told which flaw the slot plants, or whether the other checker was right. Assembly
   silently replaced a checker verdict that equalled the key, while the trace argued for the original. Fixed: the brief
   names the flaw and its definition, and assembly refuses a checker verdict equal to the key and a trace that names
   another verdict.
5. **The critic's refusals, adjudicated by R&D on 40 sampled items:**
   - TRAIT_MISMATCH (20): writer 15, critic 3, both 2. The refusals were mostly right. In C and D the writer let a
     silence call or the pressure turn decide the step, whatever the slot. Fixed on the writer side: the brief names
     the step each trait decides, with the defaults that belong to other traits.
   - B ANSWER_MISMATCH (20): check 14, writer 5, critic 1. The comparator needed a near-exact match to a free-text key,
     and the critic was shown the writer-facing pattern instruction. Fixed on the writer side: B keys are closed (a
     label, a value, a list or a map) and a puzzle has exactly one solution. The comparator and the critic prompt
     change in the critic-side release.
   - Six wrong keys rode inside these refusals (cannot_tell or ESCALATE errors, and open puzzles keyed as closed).
     The trait check stays gating.
6. **Content (R&D's read of the accepted rows).** The fixes are all in the brief:
   - T08 escalates by advice the person would act on, not by topic.
   - Labelled sides must be taught in the reply.
   - T11: acceptance needs a supporting line. T12: compare against the version the statement names.
   - Verdict-E prompts may not state what the material says.
   - Kind A is situational, and a kind-A reply may not open with a stock phrase.
   - Near-duplicates got past check_against because each chunk was its own process. One process with interleaving
     restores the check across the whole run.
